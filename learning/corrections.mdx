A correction should make the right response clear and give the student another chance to produce it. It should not become a second lecture.

Crush AP bases its core correction flow on the correction procedure in *Connecting Math Concepts*.

## The core sequence

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify the mistake">
    The app gives one short diagnosis of what the response did wrong. It does not quote the student's answer or hide the correction inside encouragement.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Give the correct answer once">
    The correct response and its essential reason appear together. The student is not required to guess again before being taught.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Repeat the missed task">
    The app re-asks the task. A correct response is the evidence that the correction landed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Return later when needed">
    Important skills reappear through delayed retests and cumulative review with fresh variants.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Why the answer comes before the retry

An unguided retry can measure persistence, but it does not necessarily teach. The CMC procedure is more direct: tell the right answer, then repeat the task that was missed.

The re-ask matters as much as the answer. Without it, the student can move on after only reading the correction. With it, the learner completes the event by producing the response.

<Info>
  Reinforcement does not mean saying the same content three ways. The student's answer remains visible, the correct answer appears once, and the prompt is repeated without extra commentary.
</Info>

## Corrections arrive in useful bundles

The first bundle contains the complete correction: diagnosis, answer, and why. One Next action opens the second bundle, the repeated task and its input.

This keeps the answer immediate while avoiding a panel that reveals the correction and retry all at once.

## The flow adapts to the response type

| Surface | Correction shape |
| --- | --- |
| Short answer or choice | Brief diagnosis, correct answer once, then the task again |
| Sketch | In-view diagnosis and rule, followed by a redraw |
| Table | Repair the highlighted cells first; reveal the completed table only if repair also misses |
| Exam-practice choice | Brief diagnosis and correct choice, followed by the existing scaffolded correction |

Tables deliberately use a repair-first exception. Showing a completed table before the student repairs its cells made the interaction less useful in product testing, so that surface keeps a different order.

## Support and mastery are separate

Correction guarantees a path forward. It does not automatically turn the original response into evidence of independent mastery. The app keeps correction, lesson accounting, and later review as separate responsibilities.
