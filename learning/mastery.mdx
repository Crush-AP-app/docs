Finishing a page does not show that a student can use an idea. Crush AP ties progress to student production: choosing, calculating, explaining, drawing, and running complete problem-solving routines.

## Lessons move from model to production

A typical sequence has four parts:

1. A worked example makes the expert decision process visible.
2. Guided responses isolate important steps and distinctions.
3. Less-supported examples ask the learner to run more of the routine.
4. Scored questions test whether the learner can produce the target performance.

The first independent test should match the operation taught in the final model. Difficulty can grow after the basic production is established.

## Passing requires performance

Scored questions contribute to the lesson result. Students can make some mistakes and still demonstrate the target skill, but repeated misses or missing coverage can fail the lesson.

A failed lesson is not a dead end. It returns with another opportunity to learn and respond. What it does not do is award mastery for clicking through a reveal.

## Fresh variants protect the meaning of a pass

Review and retest questions draw from banks of interchangeable, human-reviewed variants. The surface details change while the intended operation remains the same.

This reduces the chance that a correct response reflects memory for one answer rather than control of the skill.

## Mastery must survive time

A lesson pass is the start of retention, not the end. The skill enters a spaced schedule, receives evidence from later work, and can return to active teaching after a confirmed gap.
