Direct Instruction is often mistaken for a teacher talking while students listen. That is not the model Crush AP follows.

For us, Direct Instruction means that the explanation, examples, questions, and corrections are deliberately engineered so the intended idea is the easiest reasonable interpretation. Students respond constantly. The teaching is explicit, but the learning is active.

## Three commitments

  <Card title="Be explicit" icon="bullseye">

## Explicit teaching is a path to independence

A novice cannot reliably discover an expert routine from a finished solution. Too many decisions are hidden: what to notice, which quantity matters, which representation to build, and which rule applies.

Crush AP makes those decisions visible. Early examples show the complete reasoning. Later examples remove support in a controlled way. Independent work asks students to run the same routine on new cases.

The sequence is explicit first, independent later. Skipping the first half often creates hidden dependence on guessing, hints, or memorized surface patterns.

## The adopted design standard

Crush AP has adopted four categories from Siegfried Engelmann's *Rubric for Identifying Authentic Direct Instruction Programs* as a design constitution: presentation of information, tasks, exercises, and organization of content. Among other requirements, the system should:

- use clear and concise wording;
- introduce only the vocabulary needed now;
- assume only knowledge already taught;
- keep the amount of new information and memory burden small;
- sample the range of cases students must handle;
- avoid accidental clues that reveal the answer;
- use varied examples to reveal sameness and minimal pairs to reveal differences;
- make each task directly test what the teaching prepared;
- keep each app lesson to one manageable learning target;
- organize prerequisites so earlier mastery predicts later success.

These are not just writing preferences. They shape lesson templates, generation prompts, quality checks, and product behavior.

## What this does not mean

Direct Instruction does not mean that every idea is delivered as a rule. When a pattern is easier to understand from examples, the examples should do the teaching without an unnecessary lecture.

It also does not mean that every student sees only one path forever. Support changes with the task and the learner's response. What stays fixed is the obligation to make the intended learning clear and testable.
